Know when a post about Brazil's voting machines looks like disinformation.

Urna Guard adds an alert badge to posts on X (Twitter) that repeat already-debunked rumors about Brazil's electronic voting machine (urna eletrônica) and explains why. It never hides or reports anything.

Note: the badge, popup and welcome screens follow your browser language (English or Brazilian Portuguese). The detailed signal names inside “Technical details” are in Portuguese.

How it works

The extension only reads what is already on your screen and computes two scores: one for disinformation and one for suspicious accounts.

Caution why?

The post contains a known, already-debunked narrative about the voting machines.

High risk why?

A strong narrative posted by an account with several signs of being fake or acting in coordination.

No badge

Fact-checks, debunks, irony ("they say that…") and negations ("there was no fraud") lower the alert. No post is ever hidden.

📝 Text signals

It covers 49 weighted narratives in Portuguese. The text is cleaned of tricks such as fr4ud3, f.r.a.u.d.e, fraaaude, invisible characters and typos. Quoted posts, link cards and image descriptions are also read.

👤 Account signals

Usernames full of digits, a default photo, a generic or emoji-heavy name, a "backup account" bio, a new account, near-identical text across many accounts, the same link repeated and bursts of posts.

Privacy and LGPD

Nothing leaves your browser. Read the full privacy policy.

  • 100% local: settings and alerts stay in your browser's storage only.
  • No network: nothing is sent to servers. No telemetry, no trackers.
  • No passwords or tokens: the extension only reads the page you are already viewing.
  • You are in control: delete everything in one click.
  • It never hides posts and never reports anyone.
  • It does nothing on X by itself: no liking, replying, blocking or following.
  • Exports contain usernames and post text, which are personal data under Brazil's LGPD. Handle those files responsibly.
  • This website uses no cookies, trackers or external services either.

Limitations and disclaimer

These are automated hints, not proof. Urna Guard can be wrong both ways. Do not use the badges to harass, expose or mass-report anyone. When in doubt, check the TSE "Fato ou Boato" page (in Portuguese).
  • It doesn't understand context like a person does. Sarcasm and new rumors can slip through.
  • Account age and bio are only read when the profile is on screen.
  • Coordination detection only covers the posts you have seen.
  • It doesn't read text inside images without descriptions, or videos.
  • X changes its site code often, so the extension may need updates.
  • Real accounts can also have digits in the username or a default photo.
  • Narratives are tuned for Brazilian Portuguese content.

Frequently asked questions

What do the badge colors mean?

Yellow (Caution): the text repeats a common misinformation claim about the voting machines. Red (High risk): on top of that, the account shows signs of automation or coordinated activity. Posts without signals get no badge. Click why? on the badge for a plain-language explanation.

How do I make it stricter or more relaxed?

Click the Urna Guard icon in the browser toolbar and pick a sensitivity: Low (fewer alerts), Medium (recommended) or High (more alerts, more false positives). The on/off switch and today's counters are there too.

Is it safe to install outside the store?

The extension only asks to store data locally and only runs on x.com and twitter.com. The code is open and readable. Even so, only install extensions from sources you trust, and prefer the official stores once it's listed there.

I see a warning about "developer mode" extensions. Is that normal?

Yes. Chrome and Edge warn about manually installed extensions. You can dismiss it.

Does it work on phones?

No. Mobile browsers don't accept this kind of extension. Use it on a computer (Windows, Mac or Linux).

Does it work on Brave, Opera or Vivaldi?

Yes. Use the Chrome package and follow the Chrome steps, with brave://extensions, opera://extensions or vivaldi://extensions as the address.

Why does it disappear in Firefox when I close the browser?

Firefox only keeps extensions signed by Mozilla. Until the signed version is out, installing through about:debugging is temporary.

How do I update?

Download the new file, replace the folder's contents and click ↻ (reload) on the Urna Guard card on the extensions page. Store installs will update automatically.

How do I uninstall?

On the extensions page, click Remove on the Urna Guard card. Its local data is deleted along with it.

Is a post with a badge necessarily false?

No. The badge shows signals that often appear in disinformation. Always check official sources.
